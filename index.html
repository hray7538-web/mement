Anyone. Healthy. For a long time.

Medicine does not have to be expensive.

That is all medicine has ever been trying to do. Anyone is the part we are working on: without insurance, without much money, in your own language, at two in the morning.

The way we do it: an agent runs our clinics. People meet patients and take responsibility. Nothing else is a person's job.

We own three clinics in Hong Kong — Tsim Sha Tsui, Causeway Bay, Mong Kok. Since late July the agent has run the front of the business. It answers customers in ten languages, quotes every price, knows which branch has which machine, sends an SMS code and verifies it, books, changes and cancels. It watches all three clinics through the day and tells the staff when someone has been waiting too long, or when no therapist is free and one has to come back from break. Before morning it reconciles the card terminals against the ledger.

On 20 August someone wrote to us in Cantonese, was answered, verified and booked, with no person involved. She came in the following day, had the treatment, and paid at the clinic.

Last week, 14–20 August, across the three clinics: 88 to 112 customer conversations a day handled by the agent · 20 to 23 times a day it said it did not know and handed over · 387 alerts that someone had waited too long · 147 times it asked for staff to be moved. Counted from the operations channel it posts to.

What it does not do

It does not decide whether a treatment suits you. It does not say how often you should come, or whether it will hurt. Every one of those goes to the doctor or the therapist. About twenty times a day it stops and says it does not know.

It isn't in our catalogue, so I won't guess.

Whether it suits you personally is not something I can decide. The doctor will look.

Whatever you prefer. I won't push.

The last one is not new. Shinebeam's promise in Korea, written years earlier, is that we do not perform excessive procedures.

Why we do it this way

A doctor's day is mostly not medicine. It is forms, orders, records, checking, billing. That work was priced as medicine, which is a large part of why medicine costs what it does. It is also why one doctor can only look after so many people.

Move that work and what is left is the part only a person can do — being there, deciding, and carrying the responsibility — and it can be done for more people, earlier. Earlier is the whole point: a thing caught early is smaller, cheaper and more likely to go away.

The oath a physician takes is to help everyone who comes. Everyone. It has never been possible to keep, not because doctors did not mean it but because a day only holds so many people and one head only holds so much. Those two limits are what we are working on.

Lower cost is not the goal. It is how you reach anyone. If it is expensive people do not come, and if they do not come they come late, and late is the expensive kind of medicine and the kind that does not work as well.

Where we came from

Korean aesthetic medicine. Fifteen years, two brands — MUSE Clinic and Shinebeam — in Korea, Hong Kong and Vietnam. It is the industry the rest of the world copies; last week a customer in Hong Kong wrote to us in English asking for Korean botox.

Hong Kong is where we own and operate the clinics ourselves. That is why every number below is from Hong Kong and not from Korea. Every price is published on that site and it is what you pay.
