We own and run aesthetic-medical clinics. Three of them, in Hong Kong. We are moving to the United States.

We come out of Korean aesthetic medicine. Fifteen years, two brands, about forty clinics carrying them across the country. It is the industry the rest of the world copies — yesterday a customer in Hong Kong wrote to us in English asking for Korean botox.

Hong Kong is where we run it ourselves. Three clinics: we own them, we staff them, we carry the risk. That is why every number on this page comes from Hong Kong and not from Korea.

The person who started this is a physician and still runs the clinics.

Medicine is expensive because most of what people did in a clinic was never medicine — and it was priced as if it were. We are not automating care. We are doing the part that was never care.

Yesterday

Someone wrote to us in Cantonese. The machine answered her, found the treatments she was asking about, sent a code to her phone, verified her, and booked her. No person was involved. She came in the following day, had the treatment, and paid at the clinic.

Here is some of what the machine said to her.

Whatever you prefer. I won't push.

It isn't in our catalogue, so I won't guess.

Whether it suits you personally is not something I can decide. The doctor will look.

It quotes every price. It books. It takes payment. It verifies your phone. It answers in ten languages.

It has never once made a medical judgement.

The same machine watches all three clinics. Yesterday it flagged a customer who had been waiting forty-five minutes, and asked a therapist on break to come back because no one was free. It reconciles the day's card terminals against the ledger before morning.

Roughly twenty times a day it raises its hand and says it does not know.

That is not a failure rate. It is the line holding.
